# Cora Full structural-recovery diagnostic (v2)

This diagnostic counts **full-graph reference structures recovered across
independently reshuffled epochs**. It does not train a model, estimate predictive
accuracy, or measure training convergence. The pinned configuration is
[`configs/corafull_recovery_v2.json`](configs/corafull_recovery_v2.json): Cora
Full (19,793 nodes), ten reshuffling seeds `0,...,9`, 200 epochs, `K=32` with
`q=2,4,8,16` for all three structure families, plus `K=64,q=8` for cellular
recovery. The latter is the selected Cell TopoTune setting in the current
manuscript hyperparameter table. The selected-model annotations in the plot
code are tied to that table and **must be rechecked if its results change**.

The old Cora (2,708 nodes, `K=64`) plots are not Cora Full evidence. This v2
runner refuses a 2,708-node graph or a partition inconsistent with the pinned
configuration. No existing Cora sweep artifact is silently reused.

## Inputs and ordering

Obtain the **exact processed Cora Full graph** used by the experiments. Save its
integer `edge_index` as a `(2,E)` NumPy `.npy` array **in the original column
order**. Sorting or recreating the same undirected edge set is insufficient:
NetworkX's selected cycle basis can depend on insertion order. For each `K`,
provide the saved native partition arrays `partptr.npy`, `indptr.npy`,
`indices.npy`, and `perm_to_global.npy`, a training mask in permuted-node order,
and the corresponding independently saved sidecar and provenance JSON. The
sidecars must encode the graph, edge-order, CSR-order, and partition-assignment
hashes expected by the preflight. A path labelled `saved_experiment` is not
proof of the historic training partition: compare its assignment digest to
the actual training artifact. Otherwise label the source `regenerated`, and
do not claim historical partition identity.

The runner reconstructs each induced graph batch using the native permuted CSR
node and neighbour order. It retains **every original edge** among selected
nodes, including cross-cluster edges. It converts selected clusters into local
node IDs before asking NetworkX for the batch-local cycle basis, then maps
selected cycle boundaries back to global IDs. For the reference universe, it
builds the full graph from the original processed `edge_index` order, selects
one full-graph cycle basis, and retains cycles of length at most nine. It does
not enumerate all simple cycles. Clique references are all filled graph
triangles. Hypergraph references are centre-indexed closed one-hop
neighbourhoods, so two centres with identical memberships remain separate
reference hyperedges.

## What each quantity means

For each frozen full-graph reference structure, its **cluster span** is the
number of distinct partition clusters containing its supporting nodes. A
reference is *observable* at `q` if its span is at most `q`. This determines a
fixed analytical ceiling but does not say it appears in a given batch.
Theoretical per-epoch probability depends on `(K,q,span)` and assumes all
`K` clusters are active and `q` divides `K`. The runner rejects incompatible
partitions rather than quietly substituting an effective `K`.

`reference_summary.csv` reports the total number of frozen references per
family. `span_histogram.csv` gives counts by `(K,family,span)`.
`theory.csv` gives analytical expected cumulative coverage and Bernoulli
entropy at every integer epoch from `T=0` to `200`. Both use **all full-graph
references** as their denominator, not just observable references.

`observations.csv` has one row per `(K,q,seed,epoch,family,measurement)`.
`recovered_count` is the cumulative number of distinct frozen reference IDs
seen by that seed and epoch; repeated sightings do not increase it.
`reference_count` is the fixed all-reference denominator.
`coverage = recovered_count / reference_count`. `observable_count` is the
number with span at most `q`, so `observable_count / reference_count` is the
observable ceiling. New local cycles absent from the frozen reference are
**not** credited as recovered reference cells.

The cellular family has two separate measurements. `support_available`
credits a frozen reference cycle when all of its supporting clusters occur in
one group. `actual_basis_recovery` credits it only if the selected native
batch-local cycle basis contains that **same boundary identity**. The second
count can be smaller than the first because recomputing a cycle basis on an
induced subgraph need not select the restriction of the global basis.
Hypergraph and simplicial rows use `support_available`, with the full
neighbourhood membership additionally checked in the hypergraph case. The
analytical line models support availability. It must not be presented as a
prediction of actual cellular basis selection.

The ten seeds represent ten **independent reshuffling schedules of the same
fixed graph, partition, and reference family**. They are not ten data splits
or ten model training runs. `summary.csv` reports their mean coverage and
sample standard deviation (`ddof=1`) at each epoch, never a population SD,
standard error, or confidence interval. `sample_every=5` controls displayed
markers and SD whiskers only; the recovery curves, observations, and summary
retain every integer epoch.

`entropy_corafull_k32` is analytical structural-recovery entropy in natural
units per **full-graph reference**, not entropy of labels or predictions.
Its peak and final decay below 1% of that peak are numerical summaries of
the same analytical curve. A final decay is the last threshold crossing after
any later rise, not the first temporary dip. Its computed epochs may exceed
the 200 simulated epochs and are explicitly marked as extrapolations, not
observed recovery or training convergence. `figure_metadata.json` records
whether each milestone resolved and its reason otherwise. Resolved milestone
epochs are included as points on the plotted curve and in
`plot_source_data.csv`.

## Audit source rows and regression checks

For any panel, filter `plot_source_data.csv` by `figure`, `K`, `q`, `family`,
`measurement`, and `stat`. `measurement=theory_support` is the analytical
expected cumulative fraction. `support_available` and
`actual_basis_recovery`, with `stat=mean`, are the empirical fractions.
`sample_sd_low` and `sample_sd_high` are plotted whisker endpoints, not raw
per-seed counts. The entropy curve uses
`measurement=analytical_entropy, stat=nats_per_reference`.
`manifest_sha256` ties each source row to the validated manifest. Compare a
point to `observations.csv` by summing the ten per-seed integer numerators,
dividing each by `reference_count`, then computing mean and sample SD; compare
the analytical point to `span_histogram.csv` and the `(K,q,span)` formula.
